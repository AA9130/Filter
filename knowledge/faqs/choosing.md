# FAQs — choosing

## Do I need an RO water purifier in Dubai?

It depends on what your water actually measures, which is why the answer should never come from a website. If your drinking water tastes salty, flat or metallic, or your building is on an older riser or a tank you do not control, RO is the treatment that addresses dissolved content rather than just particles and chlorine. If your only complaint is a chlorine smell or visible sediment, a carbon and sediment filter does the job for less money and wastes no water. We test TDS and hardness on site, show you the reading, and tell you which of the two your water justifies.

## What is the difference between RO and UV water purification?

They solve different problems and are often fitted together. Reverse osmosis pushes water through a semi-permeable membrane and removes dissolved content — salts, metals, the mineral load behind a salty or flat taste. UV passes water under an ultraviolet lamp and inactivates living organisms — bacteria, viruses, cysts — without removing anything dissolved and without changing the taste. So RO changes what the water is made of; UV changes whether what is living in it can reproduce. If your concern is taste and TDS, you need RO. If your concern is a storage tank or tanker delivery, you need UV. Many UAE villas need both.

## Do I need a water softener as well as an RO purifier?

Often, yes, because they treat different points in the property. An RO purifier treats one tap — the water you drink and cook with. A softener treats the whole supply and removes the calcium and magnesium that scale your geyser, kettle, washing machine, shower glass and taps. Fitting RO does nothing for limescale anywhere else in the property, and fitting a softener does not make water suitable for drinking on its own. If you have both a taste complaint and white scale on your fittings, you are looking at two devices, not one.

## Do I need a whole-house water filter?

A whole-house — or point-of-entry — filter is worth it when your complaint is about water you do not drink: sediment in the washing machine, a chlorine smell in the shower, rust marks in a bath, scale on every fitting. It treats every outlet in the property, so it protects appliances and plumbing rather than just improving one glass of water. It is usually not the right first purchase for an apartment, where you rarely control the incoming supply, there is no plant room, and building management has to approve any work on the riser. Villas are where it earns its place.

## What water purifier is suitable for an apartment?

An under-sink RO system, in almost every case. It fits inside the sink cupboard, needs no work on the building's riser and no permission beyond your own tenancy, and treats the one outlet that matters. If the cupboard is genuinely tight, a tankless unit takes less depth because it has no storage vessel to house. In towers where mains pressure at your floor is weak, the system needs a booster pump — a detail worth confirming before purchase, because a starved RO membrane produces water slowly and wears out early.

## What water filter is suitable for a villa?

Villas usually need treatment at two points. At the point of entry, a sediment pre-filter — ideally a self-cleaning one, because tanker and tank-fed supply carries sand and rust that will otherwise block a cartridge quickly — followed by a carbon or multimedia stage, and a softener if hardness is the complaint. At the point of use, an under-sink RO for drinking water, with a UV stage if the villa is fed from a tank or a tanker rather than straight off the mains. The specific configuration should follow a water test, not a package: paying for stages your water does not need is the most common way villa owners overspend.

## Is a tankless RO better than one with a storage tank?

Neither is better; they trade different things. A tanked system stores several litres, so it delivers a fast flow on demand from a smaller, cheaper pump — but the tank takes cupboard space, water sits in storage between draws, and the tank's air charge is one more thing to maintain. A tankless system purifies as you draw, so nothing is stored and it occupies far less depth — but it needs a higher-output membrane and a pump to deliver a usable flow rate, which raises the purchase price. Tight apartment cupboard: tankless. Large family drawing several litres at once: tanked.

## Is a carbon filter enough, or do I need RO?

A carbon filter improves what you can smell and taste — chlorine, taste, odour — and a sediment stage removes what you can see. Neither removes dissolved salts and metals, because those pass straight through. So if your water reads high on TDS or tastes salty, carbon will not fix it. If your water reads low on TDS and the only complaint is a chlorine smell, carbon fixes it at a fraction of the cost, wastes no water and needs no drain connection. Measuring first is what tells you which situation you are in, and it takes ten minutes.
