# FAQs — maintenance

## How often should RO filters be replaced?

As a working schedule: sediment and carbon pre-filters every 6 months, the RO membrane every 2 to 3 years, the post-carbon or mineral stage every 12 months, and a UV lamp every 12 months whether or not it still glows. Those are starting points, not rules — the real interval depends on your measured TDS, your household's daily draw and how much sediment your building's storage puts through the pre-filters. A villa on tanker supply can exhaust a sediment cartridge in half the time an apartment does.

## How often should an RO membrane be replaced?

Typically every 2 to 3 years in UAE conditions. A membrane does not fail suddenly — it loses rejection gradually, so the honest test is a TDS reading rather than the calendar. If the purified water climbs towards a fifth of the feed water's TDS, or the system takes noticeably longer to fill, the membrane is near the end of its useful life. Membranes also fail early for a reason worth finding: skipped pre-filter changes let sediment and chlorine reach the membrane, and chlorine destroys the standard thin-film composite element.

## How much water does an RO system waste?

A residential RO system sends some water to the drain to carry away what the membrane rejects — the ratio depends on the system, the feed pressure and the membrane's condition, and is commonly a few litres to the drain for each litre purified. It is not wasted in the sense of being contaminated; it is ordinary water carrying a higher concentration of what was rejected. A well-matched pump and a healthy membrane improve the ratio noticeably, and a starved or fouled membrane worsens it — which is another reason a system that suddenly drains more is telling you something.

## What does a water filter AMC include?

An annual maintenance contract bundles the scheduled visits, the consumables listed in the tier you choose, sanitisation, leak testing, labour and call-out into one annual fee — and, importantly, puts the responsibility for remembering on us rather than you. We contact you when a service is due. The reason it matters more in the UAE than in a temperate climate is that a skipped pre-filter change here does not just degrade taste; it lets sediment and chlorine reach an expensive membrane.

## Does a water softener make my water salty?

No. An ion-exchange softener uses salt to regenerate its resin, but the salt is flushed to the drain during regeneration rather than passing into your supply. What ends up in the water is a small amount of sodium exchanged for the calcium and magnesium that were removed — an amount you cannot taste. If your softened water does taste salty, something is wrong: usually a regeneration cycle that is not draining properly, or a valve that has failed and is bypassing brine into the service line. That is a fault worth calling about, not a characteristic of softening.

## Why does a UV lamp need replacing if it still lights up?

Because a UV lamp's output falls long before it stops glowing. Disinfection depends on delivering a dose — intensity multiplied by exposure time — and a lamp near the end of its rated life can still look identical while delivering too little ultraviolet to inactivate anything reliably. That is why the interval is annual and based on running hours rather than on whether the lamp works. The quartz sleeve matters too: scale or film on the sleeve blocks the light before it reaches the water, so it is cleaned or replaced at the same visit.
