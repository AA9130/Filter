# Hard Water and Limescale in the UAE: Causes and Fixes

**What causes limescale in UAE homes and how do I stop it?**

Limescale is dissolved calcium and magnesium coming out of solution — when hard water evaporates or is heated, those minerals stay behind as the white, chalky deposit on shower glass, taps and inside a kettle. It is not dirt and it is not a sign the water is unsafe. Because it forms everywhere the water goes, nothing fitted at the kitchen sink changes it. Reducing hardness at the point of entry, with an ion-exchange softener, is the only treatment that stops it forming rather than cleaning it up afterwards.

## Key facts

- Limescale is dissolved hardness minerals, deposited when water evaporates or is heated
- It forms at every outlet, so point-of-use filters cannot address it
- An ion-exchange softener at the point of entry is the treatment that prevents it forming
- Scale on heating elements is both a failure risk and a running-cost increase

## Where you are seeing it, and why there

The pattern of where scale appears tells you exactly what is happening, and it is always the same two triggers: evaporation and heat.

### Shower glass and taps

Water evaporates and leaves the minerals behind. This is the most visible symptom and the one people notice first.

### Inside the kettle

Heat drives the minerals out of solution onto the element. A furred element is the clearest possible demonstration of what is dissolved in your water.

### Geyser and washing machine

The same process, on the components you cannot see. This is where hardness costs real money, because a scaled heating element runs less efficiently and eventually fails.

### Glassware and laundry

Spotting on glasses, stiff towels, and needing more detergent to lather. Hard water and soap do not get on.

### Skin and hair after showering

The tight, dry feeling many UAE residents assume is the climate. Some of it is the extra soap hard water demands.

## Why your kitchen filter has not helped

This is the most common misunderstanding we encounter, and it costs people the most. An under-sink filter — even a very good RO system — treats water at one tap. The water going to your shower, your geyser and your washing machine is untouched, so the scale continues to form exactly as before.

It is worth being blunt about it: if your complaint is limescale and someone recommends an under-sink RO, they have not addressed your problem. RO is excellent at what it does. What it does is not this.

## What an ion-exchange softener actually does

A softener is a vessel of resin with a control valve on top. Hard water passing through gives up its calcium and magnesium to the resin and takes on a small amount of sodium in exchange. When the resin's capacity is used up, the valve draws brine from a salt tank through the resin, displacing the accumulated hardness, and flushes the lot to the drain.

Two consequences worth knowing. First: the salt is flushed away, not passed into your supply — softened water does not taste salty, and if yours does, something has genuinely failed and is worth calling about. Second: the sizing depends on both your measured hardness and your actual demand. A softener specified from household size alone is specified from half the information, and it will either regenerate constantly or deliver hard water part of the time.

## Getting the installation right

### Protect the resin

A sediment and carbon stage ahead of the softener costs little and materially extends resin life. On a tank-fed or tanker-fed property, sediment reaching the resin bed is difficult to recover from.

### Take the irrigation off first

If a landscaping line shares the incoming supply, branch it before the softener. Plants gain nothing from softened water and the exchange capacity is simply wasted.

### Plan the drain and the salt

Regeneration needs a drain. Salt needs carrying to the tank. Both are much easier to solve at installation than to live with afterwards.

### Set the valve to your reading

Leaving factory defaults in place is the most common reason a correctly sized softener disappoints. Commissioning includes programming it to your measured hardness and usage.

## What to be sceptical of

Hardness is a large enough problem in the UAE that it attracts a lot of confident marketing. Two things to hold at arm's length.

Physical, magnetic and electronic 'descalers' claim to change how scale forms without removing hardness. The evidence for them is much weaker than the advertising suggests, and they do not reduce hardness — a hardness test after installation reads the same as before. If a device cannot show a change in a hardness reading, be careful about what you are being asked to believe.

And be sceptical of any recommendation made without a hardness measurement. It takes minutes, it determines the resin volume, and without it the sizing is guesswork.
