# How Often Should RO Filters Be Replaced in the UAE?

**What is the replacement schedule for RO filters and membranes?**

As a working schedule: sediment and carbon pre-filters every 6 months, the RO membrane every 2 to 3 years, the post-carbon or mineral stage every 12 months, and a UV lamp every 12 months. Those are starting points, not rules. In the UAE, sediment loading from building and villa storage tanks is higher than manufacturer schedules assume, so pre-filters often need attention sooner — and on a tanker-fed property, considerably sooner. The honest interval comes from what the removed cartridges actually look like on your property.

## Key facts

- Sediment and carbon: about 6 months, sooner on tank-fed or tanker-fed supply
- RO membrane: typically 2 to 3 years, judged on TDS rejection rather than the calendar
- Post-carbon or mineral stage: about 12 months — this is the stage behind most taste complaints
- UV lamp: 12 months on running hours, whether or not it still lights

## Why one interval for the whole system is wrong

The stages age by completely different mechanisms, which is why a single service date for everything is a compromise rather than a schedule.

### Sediment cartridges block

They fill up with what they have caught. This at least announces itself as reduced flow — the only stage that gives you a warning.

### Carbon exhausts

Carbon adsorbs until it is saturated, then quietly stops working while passing water perfectly normally. There is no symptom until the chlorine taste returns — by which point chlorine has been reaching your membrane.

### Membranes lose rejection gradually

A membrane does not fail; it declines. Treated TDS creeps up and production slows. Judging it by date rather than by reading either wastes money or wastes the membrane.

### UV lamps lose output while still glowing

Output decays steadily against a dose requirement that does not. A lamp at the end of its life looks identical to a new one.

## The interval that protects everything else

If you only get one interval right, make it the carbon stage. Standard RO membranes are thin-film composite elements, and chlorine destroys them. The carbon cartridge is what stands between residual disinfectant in your supply and the most expensive part of the system.

This is why we see membranes replaced two and three times more often than they should be. The membrane is not failing early because it was a poor membrane; it is failing early because the cartridge in front of it was left in for a year.

## What makes UAE intervals shorter

### Storage tanks

Most UAE properties hold water before using it. Sediment settles in tanks and is stirred up by refilling. A sediment cartridge that lasts a year on continuous mains supply can be finished in a few months here.

### Tanker delivery

The most demanding duty. Sand and rust picked up in handling go straight into the pre-filter. On these properties a self-cleaning pre-filter at the point of entry — rinsed through a valve instead of replaced — usually pays for itself quickly.

### Heat

Higher ambient temperatures accelerate biological growth inside housings and standing water. This is why sanitising the housings at every cartridge change matters more here than in a temperate climate.

### Household size

A family of six draws through the system several times faster than a couple. The consumables do not care about the calendar; they care about litres.

## How to tell your membrane is finished

Measure, do not guess. Two indicators, both easy:

### Rising treated TDS

If the purified reading climbs towards a fifth of the feed reading, rejection has degraded materially. A unit with an on-board TDS readout gives you this for free; otherwise a technician reads it at each service.

### Slower production

A tanked system taking noticeably longer to refill, or a tankless one delivering more slowly than it used to. Check the pre-filters and the tank charge first, though — both cause the same symptom and both are far cheaper.

## A practical schedule to actually follow

Write the date on the housing, or on a label inside the cupboard door, every time a cartridge is changed. It sounds trivial; it is the difference between a schedule and an intention.

Better still, do not rely on remembering at all. The failure mode is never someone deciding to skip a service — it is six months passing while the water still comes out of the tap and nothing prompts anyone. That is the specific problem a maintenance contract solves, and it is the honest reason to consider one.
