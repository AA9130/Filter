# How to Reduce TDS in Drinking Water

**How do I lower the TDS of my drinking water at home?**

Reverse osmosis is the practical answer at home: a membrane rejects most dissolved content, typically 95 to 99% of total dissolved solids. No cartridge filter can do this, because sediment and carbon stages target particles, chlorine, taste and odour, and dissolved salts pass straight through them. Distillation also works but is impractical at household scale. Worth knowing before you start: very low TDS water tastes flat to most people, which is why a remineralising post-stage is common on UAE installations — and TDS is a blunt instrument that tells you how much is dissolved, not what.

## Key facts

- RO typically rejects 95–99% of total dissolved solids; cartridge filters reject essentially none
- TDS measures quantity dissolved, not identity — a low reading is not by itself proof of safety
- Very low TDS water tastes flat; a mineral or alkaline post-stage addresses that
- Boiling does not reduce TDS — it concentrates it as water evaporates

## What TDS is, and what it is not

Total dissolved solids is the combined concentration of everything dissolved in the water — mineral salts, metals, some organics. A handheld meter estimates it by measuring electrical conductivity, which is why it is cheap and instant.

That convenience is also its limitation. TDS is a quantity, not an identity. A reading of 300 could be largely harmless calcium and magnesium, or it could include something you would rather not drink; the meter cannot distinguish. Equally, a very low reading does not prove water is safe — it says nothing about bacteria, which have almost no effect on conductivity. If you have a specific contaminant concern, laboratory analysis is the honest route, and anyone telling you a handheld meter answers it is overselling the instrument.

## What actually reduces TDS

### Reverse osmosis — yes

The practical household answer. Water is forced against a semi-permeable membrane that rejects dissolved content and sends it to the drain. Rejection depends on feed pressure, temperature and the membrane's condition.

### Distillation — yes, but impractically

Boiling and condensing removes dissolved solids effectively. At household scale it is slow, energy-hungry and produces small volumes. It is a laboratory method, not a kitchen one.

### Deionisation — yes, but not for drinking

Ion-exchange resins can strip dissolved ions to very low levels, which is why the technique is used industrially. It is not a domestic drinking-water solution.

## What does not reduce TDS, despite common belief

### Sediment and carbon filters

They improve what you can see, smell and taste. Dissolved salts pass through unchanged. A TDS reading before and after a carbon filter is essentially identical.

### Boiling

Boiling kills organisms — genuinely useful — but as water evaporates the dissolved solids stay behind, so the concentration in what remains actually rises.

### A water softener

A softener exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium. The hardness falls; the total dissolved load does not meaningfully change. Softened water is not low-TDS water.

### Magnetic and electronic descalers

These claim to alter how scale forms. They do not reduce dissolved content, and a TDS or hardness reading after installation reads the same as before.

## The taste problem nobody warns you about

Water stripped to very low TDS tastes flat. Not bad — flat. Most people find that unpleasant enough to notice within a day, and it is the leading reason people report being unhappy with a brand-new RO system that is working perfectly.

The fix is a remineralising or alkaline post-stage, which adds a small, controlled mineral content back. It is worth deciding about deliberately rather than being defaulted into it — and worth knowing that if your RO water tastes flat and there is no mineral stage fitted, the system is not faulty.

## Getting the most out of an RO system

### Feed it enough pressure

Rejection falls and production slows on inadequate pressure. On a high floor or in an older building, a booster pump is part of the system rather than an accessory.

### Keep the carbon stage current

Chlorine destroys standard membranes. The carbon cartridge is what protects the part that does the actual TDS reduction.

### Judge the membrane by reading, not by date

Rising treated TDS is the signal. A membrane replaced on a calendar is either replaced too early or left too long.

### Measure your own feed first

If your feed TDS is already low, RO is an expensive way to solve a problem you may not have. Ten minutes with a meter answers this before you spend anything.
