# What Drives the Cost of RO Installation in Dubai?

**How much does RO installation cost in Dubai, and what affects the price?**

We do not publish a figure, because it would be wrong for most people who read it — and a published 'starting from' price is usually the cheapest possible configuration in the easiest possible kitchen. What moves the number is real: the system your water test justifies, whether a booster pump is needed for your building's pressure, whether a drinking faucet can use an existing tap hole, how far the unit sits from the drain and feed point, whether it is domestic or commercial duty, and whether ongoing servicing is included. A technician quotes on site, in writing, in AED, with no call-out fee.

## Key facts

- The specification follows a measured water test, and the specification is most of the cost
- A booster pump is a common and legitimate addition on upper floors — not an upsell
- Commercial duty changes the sizing entirely; domestic pricing does not transfer
- Compare quotes on the whole cost of ownership, including consumables, not on the install price alone

## Why we will not print a number

A price on a web page has to describe some specific configuration in some specific property. The moment you publish it, it becomes the number people expect — and the honest range for 'RO installation in Dubai' spans a simple carbon-and-sediment unit in an easy kitchen through to a modular system with a pump and a UV stage in a villa utility room.

The industry's usual answer is a 'starting from' figure, which is technically true and practically misleading: it is almost always the cheapest system in the easiest installation, and very few people end up buying it. We would rather explain what moves the number and then quote your property.

## The six things that actually move the quotation

### 1. What your water justifies

The largest factor, and the one determined by measurement rather than by preference. A high TDS reading points to RO with more stages and possibly remineralisation. A low reading with a chlorine complaint points to a carbon-and-sediment unit that costs considerably less. This is why the test comes first.

### 2. Whether a booster pump is needed

Determined by static pressure at your tap, which is a function of your floor and your building. On upper floors it is common. It is a genuine part of the system — a starved membrane produces slowly, wastes more and fails early — but it is also a line item, and you should be told why it is there.

### 3. Tanked or tankless

Tankless costs more for the same filtration. You are buying cupboard space and on-demand production, not better water. If a tanked system fits, it is the cheaper correct answer.

### 4. The installation itself

Whether a faucet can use an existing tap hole or needs one drilled in stone. How far the unit sits from the drain and the cold feed. Whether isolation valves exist or have to be added. None of these are large individually; together they are the difference between a straightforward fit and a half-day job.

### 5. Domestic or commercial duty

A cafeteria or an accommodation block is sized on measured throughput, and domestic sizing fails in weeks. Commercial systems are a different category of quotation and are not comparable to a household figure.

### 6. What happens afterwards

Consumables and servicing are the majority of the lifetime cost. A quote that is cheap on installation and expensive on cartridges is not the cheaper quote — it just presents better.

## How to compare two quotations fairly

### Ask for the reading each quote was based on

If a quotation was produced without measuring your water, it was produced without the main input. Ask what the TDS and hardness readings were and where they were taken.

### Compare stage for stage, not price for price

A cheaper system with fewer stages may be exactly right for your water — or may be missing the stage that addresses your actual complaint. The specification is the comparison; the price is the consequence.

### Get the consumable cost and interval in writing

Cartridge formats, how many stages, how often, and what each costs. This is where the lifetime difference lives, and it is the question least often asked.

### Check whether the pump is included or extra

A quote without a pump on a high floor is not cheaper; it is incomplete.

### Ask what the cartridges are

Standard formats get serviced on schedule. Proprietary ones tie you to one supplier and wait for deliveries. That is a cost even when it is not a price.

### Be sceptical of pressure

A limited-time discount on a water filter is a sales technique, not a market condition. A free water test with no obligation should be exactly that.

## What you should be able to expect from a quotation

### A measured basis

Readings taken at your tap, shown to you, with an explanation of what they mean and what they do not.

### A fixed figure in AED, in writing, before work starts

Not a verbal estimate, and not a figure that moves once the technician is in the cupboard.

### No call-out fee for the visit

Including in the case where the recommendation is that you need nothing, or that your existing system only needs a cartridge.

### The ongoing cost stated up front

What the consumables are, how often, and what a service visit involves.

## The cheapest correct answer is sometimes nothing

It is worth saying plainly, because it is the outcome no price list can accommodate. If your measured TDS is low, your water tastes fine, and your existing system just needs a cartridge, then a cartridge is the whole recommendation.

That happens often enough that it is a reason to have the water measured before you shop — and a reason to be careful with anyone who arrives with a system already chosen.
