# Why Does My RO Purifier Have Low Water Pressure?

**My RO purifier flow has dropped — what is wrong?**

In order of how often we find it: a blocked sediment or carbon pre-filter, which starves the membrane; a storage tank that has lost its air charge, so it holds water but cannot push it out; low incoming mains pressure, common on higher floors and fixable with a booster pump; a fouled membrane near the end of its life; or a kinked or partially closed feed line, often after someone has moved things under the sink. The first two account for most cases and both are quick to check — which is why replacing the membrane first is the most expensive way to guess.

## Key facts

- Blocked pre-filters and a flat tank charge cause most low-flow complaints
- A tank that has lost its air charge fills with water but cannot deliver it
- Low feed pressure needs a booster pump, not a larger membrane
- The membrane is the last thing to suspect and the most expensive to replace speculatively

## Work through it cheapest first

This order is not about saving effort. These genuinely are the most common causes, and testing them is fast.

### 1. Blocked pre-filters

A sediment or carbon cartridge that has filled up restricts flow to the membrane, and a starved membrane produces slowly. If your cartridges are past due — or you cannot remember when they were changed — this is your answer more often than not.

### 2. Storage tank air charge

A pressure tank holds purified water against a diaphragm with air behind it. Lose that charge and the tank fills but cannot push out. The tell is a tank that feels heavy and full while the faucet delivers a trickle that stops almost immediately.

### 3. Low incoming pressure

Very common on high floors and in older buildings. Measure static pressure at the cold tap. If it is low, the system was always going to underperform and the fix is a booster pump.

### 4. Fouled or exhausted membrane

Genuinely does cause slow production — but usually alongside a rising treated TDS reading. Membrane alone, with normal pre-filters, a good tank charge and adequate feed pressure, is the least common of the five.

### 5. A restricted or closed feed line

A kinked tube, or a feed valve knocked partially closed. Worth ten seconds of looking, because it happens more often than anyone admits — usually after a plumber, a pest-control visit, or a reorganisation of the cupboard.

## How to tell a tank problem from a membrane problem

The pattern of the flow distinguishes them, and it is a genuinely useful diagnostic you can do yourself.

A flat tank charge gives you a short burst of reasonable flow that dies away almost immediately, because you are only getting the water that gravity and residual pressure will give up. A failing membrane gives you a steady but slow flow that keeps going, because production is the limit rather than delivery.

If the faucet delivers a weak stream indefinitely, look at production — pre-filters, pressure, membrane. If it delivers briefly and stops, look at the tank.

## Why a booster pump is a specification, not an upsell

An RO membrane is a pressure-driven device. Below its design pressure, three things happen at once: production slows, the proportion of water sent to the drain rises, and rejection falls slightly — so you get less water, waste more, and it is marginally less well purified.

This is why we measure static pressure before quoting, and why on an upper floor a pump belongs in the original specification. A system sold without the pump it needed will be reported as faulty within months, and the customer will be told the membrane needs replacing. It does not.

## Tankless systems and low pressure

A tankless system feels low feed pressure immediately, because it has no reservoir to draw on while the membrane catches up. There is no tank charge to check either, which removes one cause from the list but makes the remaining ones more noticeable.

On a tankless unit, a sudden drop in flow points hard at the pre-filters or the pump. Many have a low-pressure warning light, which is worth trusting.

## When to stop investigating and call

### You have changed the pre-filters and it is no better

Time for readings — feed pressure, tank pressure, feed and treated TDS. Guessing past this point gets expensive.

### Flow dropped suddenly rather than gradually

Gradual decline is consumables. A sudden change is usually a valve, a pump or a kink, and it is worth finding rather than living with.

### Flow is low and the drain runs more than it used to

That combination points at the membrane or the flow restrictor and is worth diagnosing properly.
