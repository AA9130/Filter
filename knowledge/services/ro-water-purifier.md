# RO Water Purifier Systems in the UAE

A reverse osmosis purifier pushes water through a semi-permeable membrane that rejects most dissolved content — typically 95 to 99% of total dissolved solids — which cartridge filters cannot do. AquaPure supplies, installs and services domestic and commercial RO systems across the UAE, sized to a measured TDS reading and fitted with the booster pump the building's pressure requires.

## Key facts

- RO membranes typically reject 95 to 99% of total dissolved solids
- The right answer only when your water actually reads high — carbon is cheaper and wastes no water when TDS is low
- Needs a drain connection and adequate feed pressure; a starved membrane fails early
- Membrane life is typically 2 to 3 years, judged on a TDS reading rather than the calendar

## What it involves

Reverse osmosis forces water at pressure against a semi-permeable membrane. Water molecules pass through; most dissolved salts, metals and larger organic molecules do not, and are carried away to the drain in a concentrate stream. That rejection of dissolved content is what distinguishes RO from every cartridge-based filter — sediment and carbon stages target particles, chlorine, taste and odour, and dissolved salts pass straight through them.

A residential RO system is a train of stages, not a single device: sediment and carbon pre-filters that protect the membrane, the membrane itself, and a post-stage that polishes taste. Most UAE installations add a remineralising or alkaline cartridge, because water stripped to very low TDS tastes flat to most people. Systems either store purified water in a small pressure tank or purify on demand.

## Who needs it

### Households whose water tastes salty, flat or metallic

A taste that is not chlorine is usually a dissolved-solids complaint, and that is what RO addresses.

### Properties on high-TDS supply

Where a measured reading is genuinely high, no cartridge filter changes it. RO or distillation are the only practical options, and distillation is impractical at household scale.

### Apartments with no other option

You cannot treat the building's incoming supply, so treating the drinking outlet properly is the whole available strategy.

### Commercial kitchens and cafeterias

Consistent water for coffee, ice and cooking, sized to measured throughput. Domestic sizing fails in weeks under commercial duty.

## How it works

### 01 — Sediment stage

Removes particles that would otherwise abrade and block the membrane. On tank-fed properties this is the stage that does the most work.

### 02 — Carbon stage

Adsorbs chlorine, taste and odour. Critically, it protects the membrane: chlorine destroys the standard thin-film composite element, so a skipped carbon change is what kills membranes early.

### 03 — Membrane

Rejects the dissolved load. Purified water continues; the concentrate goes to the drain. Rejection depends on feed pressure, temperature and the membrane's condition.

### 04 — Post-treatment and delivery

A polishing carbon stage, and usually a remineralising cartridge so the water does not taste flat. Then either a storage tank or on-demand delivery to a dedicated faucet.

## Maintenance

### Pre-filters every 6 months

Sediment and carbon. This is the interval that protects the expensive part; missing it is the most common cause of premature membrane failure.

### Membrane every 2 to 3 years

Judged on rejection, not the date. Rising treated TDS or slower production are the signals.

### Storage tank air charge

A tank that has lost its charge holds water but cannot push it out. It presents as low pressure at the faucet and is quick to correct.

### Sanitisation at cartridge changes

Housings are opened at every service; sanitising them then costs nothing and prevents biofilm establishing inside.

## Common problems

### Slow flow or a trickle at the faucet

- **Cause**: Blocked pre-filters, a flat storage tank charge, low feed pressure, or a fouled membrane — in roughly that order of frequency.
- **Fix**: Check the pre-filters and tank charge first; both are quick. Persistent low feed pressure needs a booster pump, not a bigger membrane.

### Treated water tastes flat

- **Cause**: No remineralising stage, or an exhausted one.
- **Fix**: Fit or replace the mineral cartridge. This is a taste preference, not a fault.

### Membrane replaced far too soon

- **Cause**: Chlorine reaching it because the carbon stage was skipped or overrun.
- **Fix**: Restore the pre-filter schedule. Replacing membranes without fixing the cause repeats the expense.

### System runs constantly or drains more than it used to

- **Cause**: A failed shut-off valve, a fouled membrane, or a leaking check valve.
- **Fix**: Diagnose before replacing parts — a system that will not stop is telling you which component has failed.

## Choosing

### Measure your TDS first

RO is the right answer for a dissolved-solids problem and an expensive answer for a chlorine problem. Ten minutes of measurement decides which you have.

### Tanked or tankless

Tanked delivers a fast flow from a smaller pump but occupies cupboard space and stores water between draws. Tankless stores nothing and takes far less depth but needs a higher-output membrane and a pump. Cupboard depth and household size decide it.

### Pressure and pumping

Confirm static pressure at the tap before choosing. On high floors and in older buildings, a booster pump is part of the system rather than an accessory.

### Cartridge availability

Standard formats get serviced on schedule. Proprietary ones wait for a delivery — a real consideration outside Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

## UAE context

UAE mains water is predominantly desalinated, and desalinated water leaving a plant is low in dissolved solids. What people measure at the tap can be different, because water is blended, distributed and then commonly held in building or property storage before use.

This is why a measured reading matters more here than a general statement about the country's water. Two apartments in the same district can read differently, and the same property can read differently after a tank has been cleaned. We do not publish a TDS figure for any emirate — the only number worth specifying a system against is the one measured at your tap.
