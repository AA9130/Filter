# Water Filter System Relocation Within the UAE

Relocating a water filtration system means uninstalling it, draining and sanitising it, transporting it, and recommissioning it at the new property — with the new address surveyed first, because pressure, drain access and mounting space rarely match the old one. AquaPure handles it as a single job and fits fresh cartridges where a unit has been opened, since standing water in a transported system is not water to hand back.

## Key facts

- One job, not an uninstall and a separate install — the sequence matters
- System is drained and sanitised before transport; standing water in a moved unit is a contamination risk
- New property surveyed for pressure, drain and space before refitting
- Cartridges are usually replaced, because the housings have been opened

## What it involves

A relocation is a controlled shutdown, a move and a fresh commissioning. The shutdown matters because a system full of standing water that is then carried in a hot vehicle is not something to reconnect and drink from. The commissioning matters because the new property is a different hydraulic situation: different static pressure, a different drain arrangement, a different amount of space under a different sink.

The most common mistake is treating it as furniture — unbolt it, carry it, bolt it back. That produces the two complaints we get called back for: a leak at a joint that was disturbed and not properly reseated, and a system that now performs worse because the new property's pressure is lower than the old one's.

## Who needs it

### Anyone moving home within the UAE

A good system is worth taking. It is usually far cheaper to move than to replace, provided the move is done properly.

### Kitchen renovations

The system has to come out and go back. Doing it as a planned relocation rather than around the joinery avoids the leak that appears a week after the kitchen is finished.

### Businesses changing premises

Commercial systems are worth relocating and are also the ones where sizing to the new site's demand must be rechecked rather than assumed.

### Anyone repositioning a system in place

Moving a unit from a cramped position to a serviceable one is worth doing on its own merits.

## How it works

### 01 — Survey the destination first

Static pressure, drain access, feed point, space and mounting. Doing this before the move is what prevents arriving with a system that will not fit or will not perform.

### 02 — Shut down and prepare

Isolate, drain, sanitise, cap the connections and pack. A drained system travels safely; a full one does not.

### 03 — Refit at the new address

Mount, connect with new fittings where old ones have been disturbed, and add the isolation valves the old installation may have lacked.

### 04 — Recommission

Fresh cartridges, flush, pressure test, confirm the treated reading, and record the new configuration and next service date.

## Benefits

### Cheaper than replacing a good system

Particularly for point-of-entry and commercial equipment, where the hardware is the significant cost.

### The new property gets checked

Pressure and drain arrangement are verified rather than assumed, which is where the performance complaints come from.

### A chance to fix the original install

Missing isolation valves, an awkward mounting position, an overdue cartridge set — a relocation is the natural moment to correct all of it.

### Hygienic by procedure

Drained, sanitised, re-cartridged. Not because it sounds thorough, but because a transported system full of warm standing water is a genuine risk.

## Maintenance

### Treat the refit as a service

New cartridges, new O-rings on disturbed joints, and a full flush. The cost of doing this is small against the cost of not doing it.

### Reset the schedule

The service clock restarts at the new address, and the record should say so.

### Recheck pressure after a few weeks

Pressure in a new building can differ from what a one-off reading suggested, particularly in a partially occupied tower.

## Common problems

### Leak appears days after the move

- **Cause**: A push-fit joint disturbed and reseated onto a tube that was already cut at an angle.
- **Fix**: Cut back and reseat squarely, with new fittings where the old ones have been reused too often.

### System performs worse at the new property

- **Cause**: Lower static pressure than the previous address.
- **Fix**: This is why the destination is surveyed first. The fix is usually a booster pump, but it is much better known in advance.

### System will not fit the new cupboard

- **Cause**: Moved before measuring.
- **Fix**: Survey first. Sometimes the honest answer is that a tanked system should become a tankless one at the new address.

## Choosing

### Survey before you commit to moving it

If the destination cannot take the system, knowing that before the van arrives is worth a great deal.

### Budget for cartridges as part of the move

Not an upsell — a consequence of opening the housings. Plan for it rather than being surprised by it.

### Consider whether the system still suits you

Household size and water may both have changed. A relocation is a reasonable moment to reconsider the specification.

## UAE context

Moving within the UAE is common enough that relocation is a routine service rather than an exception, and the emirate-to-emirate variation is what makes the destination survey worthwhile: a system that worked on a mains-fed apartment riser in Dubai can be feeding from a tank in Ajman, or from a tanker-filled tank in the northern Emirates.

That changes more than pressure. A system moving onto stored or tankered supply may now need a sediment stage it never needed before, and may justify disinfection it previously did not. The equipment travels; the water it is treating does not.

## Cost

Relocation is quoted after the destination survey, since the work depends on what the new property needs — new fittings, added valves, a booster pump, or simply a straightforward refit. Cartridges are quoted as part of the job rather than added afterwards.
