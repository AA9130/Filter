## What it involves

A water filter installation is two decisions and one job. The decisions are where to treat the water — at one tap, or where it enters the property — and what to treat it for. The job is plumbing that connects a treatment system to your supply and your drain without leaking, without starving the system of pressure, and in a way that can be serviced later without dismantling a kitchen.

Point-of-use means treating one outlet, usually the kitchen cold tap, with a filter under the sink and a dedicated drinking faucet. Point-of-entry means treating everything, with a system fitted where the supply enters the property. They are not competing options: many UAE villas need both, because they solve different complaints.

## Who needs it

### Apartment residents

An under-sink system is the practical option: it fits inside your unit, needs no work on the building riser, and treats the water you actually drink. Nothing else in an apartment is usually yours to change.

### Villa and townhouse owners

You control the incoming line, so a sediment and carbon stage at the point of entry protects every tap, shower and appliance — and a drinking-water system at the kitchen sink handles the rest.

### New tenants and new owners

An inherited system that has not been serviced in years is a liability, not an asset. The first job is finding out what is fitted, what state the cartridges and membrane are in, and whether it is worth keeping.

### Restaurants, offices and accommodation blocks

Duty cycles are far higher than domestic, so sizing has to follow measured demand. Scale reduction ahead of coffee, steam and dishwashing equipment is usually more urgent than the drinking tap.

## How it works

### 01 — Water test

TDS and hardness measured at your tap, in front of you. This is what separates a specification from a guess — the same building can read very differently on different risers.

### 02 — Survey and quotation

Pressure at the tap, available space, drain and feed access, and whether a faucet can use an existing tap hole. You get a fixed written quotation in AED before anything is ordered.

### 03 — Installation

New fittings, isolation valves you can reach, and a mounting position chosen so a service visit does not mean emptying a cupboard or draining the property.

### 04 — Commission and handover

Pressure test, flush the new cartridges through, confirm the treated reading, and show you where the shut-off is. Water from a brand-new RO membrane is never handed over to drink.

## Benefits

### Sized to your water, not to a package

Paying for filtration stages your water does not need is the most common way UAE households overspend. A measured reading tells you where to stop.

### Serviceable from day one

Isolation valves, accessible housings and a sensible mounting position cost nothing extra at installation and save every service visit afterwards.

### Pressure accounted for

Static pressure is checked before quoting. A membrane starved of pressure produces slowly, sends more water to the drain and fails early — and the symptom looks like a faulty system.

### One accountable trade

The plumbing and the filtration are the same job. Splitting them between a plumber and a supplier is how leaks end up being nobody's responsibility.

## Maintenance

### Pre-filters

Sediment and carbon stages roughly every 6 months, sooner on tank-fed or tanker-fed properties where sediment loading is higher.

### RO membrane

Typically every 2 to 3 years, judged on a TDS reading rather than the calendar.

### UV lamp and sleeve

Annually, on running hours — a lamp's output falls well before it stops lighting.

### Records

We keep what was fitted and when, so the next visit starts from a known state rather than from an inspection.

## Common problems

### System fitted with no isolation valve

- **Cause**: Installed as fast as possible rather than as serviceably as possible.
- **Fix**: Adding a valve at the next service turns every future cartridge change from a plumbing job into a five-minute one.

### Drinking faucet drilled through a stone worktop unnecessarily

- **Cause**: Not checking whether an existing tap hole or sink knockout could be used.
- **Fix**: Ask before the drill comes out. In rented apartments this matters twice over.

### Drain saddle fitted badly

- **Cause**: A rushed connection to the sink waste.
- **Fix**: A correctly clamped and sealed connection. A weeping drain saddle is the leak people discover months later, by smell.

## Choosing

### Measure before you choose

TDS and hardness point to different technologies. Buying before measuring is how people end up with RO for a chlorine complaint, or a softener for a taste complaint.

### Match capacity to household size

A system that suits two people is undersized for six. The symptom is not a fault — it is slow refill and a family that stops using the filtered tap.

### Check the space honestly

Measure the cupboard, including depth behind the waste trap. This is what decides between a tanked and a tankless system more often than any specification does.

### Prefer standard consumables

A system built around common cartridge formats gets serviced on time. One built around proprietary consumables waits for a delivery — which matters a great deal outside Dubai.

## UAE context

Mains water in the UAE is predominantly desalinated and leaves the plant treated to a drinking-water standard. What installation practice has to account for is what happens after that: most UAE buildings hold water in ground or roof tanks and lift it with booster pumps, and many villas are tank-fed or tanker-fed.

That has three practical consequences. Sediment loading is higher than the plant's output would suggest, so a proper sediment stage protects everything downstream. Pressure at the tap varies enormously between floors and buildings, so it has to be measured rather than assumed. And where water has been stored before use, disinfection is a separate question from filtration — a sediment or carbon cartridge does not disinfect anything.
