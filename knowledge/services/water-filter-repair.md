# Water Filter and RO System Repair in the UAE

Most RO and filter faults come from a short list: blocked pre-filters, a storage tank that has lost its air charge, low feed pressure, a perished housing O-ring, a failed check valve or shut-off valve, or a fouled membrane. AquaPure diagnoses the cause before replacing parts, and answers an emergency line 24 hours a day. If you have a leak now, close the system's feed valve first — that stops the damage while we are on the way.

## Key facts

- Leaks and total loss of flow are treated as priority call-outs
- Low pressure is most often a blocked pre-filter or a flat tank charge, not a failed membrane
- Diagnosis comes before parts — replacing a membrane speculatively is the most expensive way to guess
- Emergency line answered 24 hours a day, every day

## What it involves

Repair work on filtration systems is mostly diagnosis. The components are simple and few, but the symptoms overlap badly: low flow at the faucet can be a cartridge, a tank, the mains pressure, a valve or the membrane, and four of those five are cheap while the fifth is not. Working from symptom to cause in the right order is the whole skill.

The other half is knowing what fails silently. A storage tank loses its air charge without any visible sign. A check valve fails and the system runs continuously. A carbon cartridge exhausts and quietly stops treating. None of those present as a broken part, which is why an inspection reads the system's pressures and readings rather than just looking at it.

## Who needs it

### Anyone with a leak under the sink

Close the feed valve, then the tank valve, then call. Under-sink leaks damage the cabinet long before water reaches a floor.

### Systems that have stopped producing

No flow or a trickle. Usually one of three cheap causes, occasionally the membrane.

### Systems that never stop

Continuous running or a drain that never quite stops means a valve has failed. It also means water is being wasted continuously.

### Systems nobody will service

Unbranded or imported units that the original supplier has abandoned. We work on them; the honest caveat is cartridge availability.

## How it works

### 01 — Take the readings

Feed pressure, feed and treated TDS, tank pressure, and where water actually appears if there is a leak. Readings separate the overlapping symptoms.

### 02 — Work cheapest cause first

Pre-filters and tank charge before membranes and pumps. Not to save time — because they are genuinely the most common causes, and testing them is fast.

### 03 — Repair, and say when it is not worth repairing

If a system is beyond economical repair, that is the finding. Continuing to charge for repairs on a system that needs replacing is the thing we most want not to be.

### 04 — Fix the cause, not just the symptom

A membrane that failed from chlorine needs the carbon schedule fixed too, or the replacement fails the same way.

## Maintenance

### Most repairs are deferred maintenance

Blocked pre-filters, exhausted carbon and perished O-rings are all schedule failures rather than component failures.

### Know where your shut-off is

The single most useful thing an owner can know. It converts a leak from damage into an inconvenience.

### Watch the drain

A system draining more than it used to is reporting a developing fault, usually before anything else changes.

### Do not run a leaking system while you investigate

The investigation is easier dry, and the damage stops immediately.

## Common problems

### Low or no flow at the faucet

- **Cause**: Blocked pre-filters, flat tank air charge, low feed pressure, fouled membrane, or a partially closed feed valve.
- **Fix**: Test in that order. The first two account for the majority.

### Leak at a housing

- **Cause**: Perished O-ring, or a housing overtightened until it deformed.
- **Fix**: New O-ring and correct torque. A replacement housing where the thread or bowl has been damaged.

### Leak at a push-fit tube

- **Cause**: Tube not fully seated, or disturbed when something was moved under the sink.
- **Fix**: Cut back and reseat squarely. A tube cut at an angle will not seal reliably.

### System runs continuously

- **Cause**: Failed shut-off or check valve, or a storage tank that cannot build pressure.
- **Fix**: Identify which — the two present almost identically and the parts cost very different amounts.

### Noise or hammering when the pump runs

- **Cause**: Air in the line, a failing pump, or a pump mounted rigidly against a cabinet panel.
- **Fix**: Bleed, isolate the mounting, or replace the pump — in that order of cost.

## Choosing

### Repair or replace

The honest test is the cost of the repair against the remaining life of the rest of the system. A new membrane in a system with perished housings and a dead pump is money in the wrong place.

### Insist on the cause, not just the fix

A fault that has an explanation will not repeat. A fault that was only patched will.

### Emergency versus scheduled

Leaks and no-flow are emergencies. A taste change is not, and booking it as routine gets it a longer, more thorough visit.

## UAE context

Two conditions make certain faults more common here. Sediment loading from tanks and tanker delivery blocks pre-filters faster, so 'low flow' is a pre-filter far more often in the UAE than the fault statistics from a temperate mains-fed market would suggest.

And heat. High ambient temperatures accelerate the degradation of plastic housings, O-rings and push-fit tubing, particularly in a cabinet next to an oven or in an unshaded plant room. It is also why a system left standing in an empty property over a summer often needs more than switching back on.
