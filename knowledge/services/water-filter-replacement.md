# Water Filter and Membrane Replacement Across the UAE

Sediment and carbon pre-filters are typically replaced every 6 months, RO membranes every 2 to 3 years, and UV lamps annually. Those are starting points: the real interval depends on your measured water, your household's daily draw and how much sediment your property's storage puts through the system. AquaPure replaces cartridges and membranes on all major brands, including systems fitted by other companies, and records what was fitted so the next visit starts from a known state.

## Key facts

- An exhausted cartridge keeps passing water — there is no symptom until quality has already dropped
- Skipping carbon changes is the leading cause of premature RO membrane failure, because chlorine destroys the membrane
- Intervals should follow measured usage and loading, not a fixed calendar
- Housings are sanitised while open, which costs nothing extra and prevents biofilm establishing

## What it involves

Replacement is a service visit, not a product purchase. The cartridges are the consumable part; the value is in changing the right ones at the right time, sanitising the housings while they are open, checking the parts that fail silently — the tank's air charge, the pump, the check valves — and leaving a record of what is fitted and when it is next due.

The stages age differently, which is why a single interval for the whole system is wrong. Sediment cartridges block, which at least announces itself as reduced flow. Carbon exhausts, which announces nothing at all: it adsorbs until saturated, then passes chlorine and taste straight through while looking identical. Membranes lose rejection gradually. UV lamps lose output while still glowing.

## Who needs it

### Anyone whose filters are past due

Including — especially — anyone who is not sure when they were last changed. That uncertainty is itself the answer.

### People whose water has started tasting different

Returning chlorine taste is usually exhausted carbon. Flat taste is usually an exhausted mineral stage. Both are cartridge answers.

### Owners of systems bought from a retailer

A large share of our replacement work is on systems bought over the counter and never serviced. They are usually straightforward once we identify the cartridge formats.

### New occupants of a property with a system already fitted

An inherited system of unknown history should be inspected, sanitised and re-cartridged before it is trusted.

## How it works

### 01 — Identify what is fitted

Cartridge formats, membrane rating, pump and housing types. On unbranded and imported systems this is the step that decides whether parts are on the van or need ordering.

### 02 — Read the system before changing anything

Feed and treated TDS, pressure, tank charge. A cartridge change that ignores a fouled membrane or a flat tank fixes nothing the customer will notice.

### 03 — Replace and sanitise

New cartridges, new O-rings where they have perished, housings sanitised while open, and everything torqued rather than overtightened — a deformed housing is a future leak.

### 04 — Flush, test and record

Flush the new media through, confirm the treated reading, pressure test the joints, and record what was fitted with the next due date.

## Benefits

### The expensive part is protected

Pre-filters exist to keep sediment and chlorine off the membrane. Changing them on time is the cheapest thing you can do for the system.

### Sanitising while open

Housings are only ever open during a service. Sanitising then prevents the biofilm problem that otherwise develops out of sight.

### Silent failures get caught

Tank air charge, check valves and pump output are all things that fail without an obvious symptom and are all checked at a service.

### A record, so the next visit is informed

Knowing what is fitted and when it was fitted turns future servicing from investigation into maintenance.

## Maintenance

### Sediment and carbon: about 6 months

Shorter on tank-fed and tanker-fed properties, and in buildings with older pipework.

### RO membrane: 2 to 3 years

Judged on rejection. Rising treated TDS or slower production are the signals to act on.

### Mineral or post-carbon: about 12 months

This is the stage behind most 'the water tastes different' calls.

### UV lamp: 12 months

On running hours. The sleeve is cleaned or replaced at the same time.

## Common problems

### Leak appears after a cartridge change

- **Cause**: A perished O-ring reused, or a housing overtightened until it deformed.
- **Fix**: New O-rings as standard and correct torque. A housing that needed force to seal was already telling you something.

### Membrane replaced but water is still poor

- **Cause**: The pre-filters were the actual problem, or the new membrane is being starved by low pressure.
- **Fix**: Diagnose before replacing. A membrane is the most expensive part to change speculatively.

### Wrong cartridge fitted

- **Cause**: Similar dimensions, different micron rating or media.
- **Fix**: Match the specification, not just the size. A coarser sediment cartridge that fits will still let the membrane down.

### Interval far too long for the property

- **Cause**: A generic six-month schedule applied to a tanker-fed villa.
- **Fix**: Set the interval from what the removed cartridges actually look like. That is the only honest calibration.

## Choosing

### Genuine or equivalent, but always to specification

What matters is the micron rating, the media and the fit. A cheaper cartridge of the correct specification is fine; a cheaper cartridge of unknown specification is not.

### Change the whole set, not one stage

Staggering changes across visits saves nothing and leaves the newest cartridge working behind an exhausted one.

### Standard formats pay off over years

Especially outside Dubai and Abu Dhabi, where a special order can add days to a service visit.

### A contract beats remembering

The failure mode is never a decision to skip a service. It is forgetting. That is what a maintenance contract actually solves.

## UAE context

Cartridge life is shorter in the UAE than manufacturer schedules assume, for a specific reason: sediment loading. Water held in ground and roof tanks, and water delivered by tanker, carries sand and rust that a mains-fed property in a temperate climate does not see. A sediment cartridge that lasts a year elsewhere can be finished in a few months here.

The second local factor is heat. Higher ambient temperatures accelerate biological growth inside housings and standing water, which is why sanitising at every cartridge change matters more here, and why a system left standing in an unoccupied property needs sanitising and flushing before use rather than simply being switched back on.
